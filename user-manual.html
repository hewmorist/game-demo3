Getting started

  1. On the Room page, choose Introductory lock or Daily lock. Both have four wheels.
  2. The introduction explains discovery from pickup order and offers AR-X-Ray Lock Model. The daily lock is a fresh listening puzzle; its Lock Model unlocks once you open it.
  3. Choose Crack Open With AI for optional AI assistance, or No Assist to solve it yourself. Either starts from zero. You can choose the assistant’s on-device voice below the dial.

The daily lock changes at midnight in America/Los_Angeles (Pacific time). Everyone receives the same date-based lock. An active session keeps its lock across midnight; return to the Room and re-enter Daily lock, or reload, to start the new day. This resets the dial.

Drag around the dial with a mouse or finger. Lift and re-grip to continue. Keep away from the centre for reliable control. Arrow right/up turns clockwise; left/down turns anticlockwise. Each key advances to the next dial click, normally one division away. Near a gate the intervening regular click is omitted and the step ends exactly at the gate, making a longer interval of between one and two divisions. Shift + arrow still moves one tenth for fine control.

Clock means clockwise; anti means anticlockwise. Read the number at the fixed top pointer. Clockwise rotation decreases the displayed number; anticlockwise increases it. Numbers wrap through zero.

Reset dial resets the wheel pack and indicators. Selecting the other lock or entering a new daily lock also resets it. Returning to the Room preserves the current mechanism, but stops AI guidance. Reloading begins a new session on the Room page, even if the previous URL opened a lock.

What the dial moves

The dial drives a chain of four separate wheels. Wheel 4 is nearest the dial; wheel 1 is farthest away. Each wheel has a gate: all four gates must line up beneath the fence before the bolt can retract.

Drive pegs push adjacent wheels. They do not pull them back when you reverse. Reversing first takes up slack, then picks up wheel 4, then 3, then 2, then 1. Wheels that have not yet been picked up stay where they were.

This lets you position the farthest wheel first, reverse to position the next wheel without moving the farthest, and repeat inward. Enough travel can pick up an already positioned wheel again and spoil its setting.

Our model has 352° of slack per connection—just under a full revolution. After reversal from a fully coupled pack, travel between 352° and 704° is the nearest-wheel-only window. An isolated gate there can identify wheel 4. A reversal from another starting configuration needs its own pickup evidence; the same window cannot be assumed blindly.

The PICKUP and GATE indicators flash on actual mechanical crossings and show the latest reading and event count.

Gate indicators deliberately omit wheel identity. During initial exploration, the AI says “Four turns to find four unique gate positions. Go clock”, then “One”, “Two”, “Three”, “Four”, “Stop”, and “Four unique gate positions found”. Only newly observed dial positions are counted: repeated positions on later turns are ignored. These numbers do not identify wheels, and the reverse isolation check does not add a fifth count. Fast turns can skip spoken counts: only the newest waiting count is kept, and counts more than one second old are dropped. During trials, mechanical gate sounds continue without spoken gate counts. Each guided wheel placement ends with a sound before the spoken confirmation. A real crossing uses the gate tone and flashes GATE. Every trial placement also flashes the separate blue POSITIONED indicator with its wheel, trial and dial reading. If no real crossing occurred at the target, the same high tone acknowledges the proposed position; only POSITIONED flashes, with no added gate count. “Wheel positioned” confirms the proposed assignment, not that its gate is correct. Mechanical sound and voice controls are independent; No AI mode suppresses guidance and spoken numbers while retaining the mechanism and optional indicators.

A gate reading depends on direction

A wheel’s gate is fixed on the wheel, but the dial number associated with it changes when the drive pegs contact from the opposite side. More connections mean a larger accumulated correction.

WheelClockwise reading → anticlockwise setting
4 · nearestSubtract 2.222… divisions
3Subtract 4.444… divisions
2Subtract 6.666… divisions
1 · farthestSubtract 8.888… divisions

For an anticlockwise reading converted to a clockwise setting, add instead. Wrap results into the dial’s 0–100 range: subtracting 4.44 from 2 gives about 97.56. Keep full precision in calculations and round only the displayed result.

Example: a clockwise gate reading of 91.89, assessed as wheel 2, gives an anticlockwise setting near 85.22. If that reading belonged to a different wheel, its correction would be different.

These are corrections to the wrapped dial number, not instructions to reverse by that small distance. A wheel cannot be pulled back through slack. You must travel far enough to pick it up from the other side, while preserving the wheels already positioned.

For a wheel n connections from the dial, clockwise → anti is: subtract n × 2.222…, modulo 100. The full slack is n × 352°; divided by 3.6° per dial division, that is n × 97.777… divisions, equivalent modulo 100 to subtracting n × 2.222…. These constants belong to this simulation.

Reading the evidence as an experiment

In the introduction, each new gate appears after just one more wheel has joined the motion. That separates the readings by pickup order. In the daily lock, several wheels are already moving when a new gate is heard, so its identity is uncertain.

The daily AI strategy collects four positions clockwise, reverses to check wheel 4 in isolation, then tests the remaining trials. Three unidentified readings have six possible assignments; the daily AI tests at most three trials in its preferred order. A failed bolt test rules out that complete trial; it does not tell you which individual wheel was wrong. The assistant announces the start and end of each numbered trial. The AI does not consult the configured answer for this search.

Daily locks are selected so the guided strategy opens on trial 2 about 60% of days, and on trials 1 and 3 about 20% each; never later than trial 3 when the search is followed correctly. These are genuine bolt tests, with no forced failures. Configurations are also checked for distinct gate readings, a successful isolated return observation, and a direct unassisted opening sequence. The AI still uses the observations from your actual turns. If extra movement makes the evidence inconclusive, restart the guided lock and follow the slow search.

Catch a gate by sound

No Assist and Dial Open With AI allow movement through gates. Gate sounds mark actual crossings; centring occurs when you reverse or try the final handle, after a verified crossing.

The GATE indicator shows the last crossing, not a continuous alignment reading. Later movement can move that wheel away again. For testing unrestricted motion through gates, use the URL override described below.

Pass-through opening

No Assist and Dial Open With AI use integer stops one to two dial divisions beyond the gates. From Reset, turn through each intended gate on its correct appearance, stop at the stated integer, then reverse. On the final row, stop and Try handle. There is no extra click or timed pause. A verified crossing is centred when that leg ends; one division of stopping error is allowed. Later turns can still disturb centred wheels. The fence drops only when all actual gates align simultaneously. Reset restarts the selected opening mode. Instructions are shown for already cracked locks; an unsolved daily lock’s answers remain hidden.

Opening the lock

AR-X-Ray Lock Model is available on the introductory lock. The daily lock’s Lock Model unlocks after you open it, with or without AI assistance. Your smart safecracking tool reconstructs the mechanism when the lock opens, with or without spoken AI guidance. The Lock Model opens automatically, ready to inspect when you dismiss the treasure celebration. The daily unlock remains during replay, guided opening, and visits to the Room in this session. A new daily lock starts locked; reloading the app starts a new session. It opens a movable panel showing the drive, four wheels, gates, fence and bolt. Drag its heading to reposition it; close it or press Escape to dismiss it. It shares the real mechanism with the dial. It does not independently move or set wheels.

Brass indicates moving wheels; green indicates aligned gates. On the introduction, AR-X-Ray Lock Model can show Gate aligned on every wheel and Fence down. Before opening the daily lock, rely on the sounds, optional pickup and gate indicators, and availability of the bolt button. The tolerance is one dial division (3.6°) on either side of each gate; merely looking close to the top notch is not enough.

Then press Retract bolt below the dial. In opening modes, Try handle is available for an attempt; it centres a verified final crossing before checking actual gate alignment. You can also retract by turning clockwise ten divisions while the gates remain aligned. If that direction immediately drives a wheel out of alignment, use the separate bolt button once aligned.

Successful opening triggers the door animation, treasure and fanfare, and reveals the result sheet. Turn mechanical sound on to hear the fanfare.

Guided discovery uses candidate holds to make positioning forgiving. No AI mode uses free wheel motion with no candidate holds. Pass-through mode centres a wheel only after verifying its gate crossing, as described above. Opening modes allow travel through the gate to the instructed stop. Reversals and extra turns can therefore change your settings.

Dial Open With AI

For an already cracked or loaded saved lock, choose Dial Open With AI. This resets the dial and starts anticlockwise. A dot marks the target integer and moves with that number on the dial: blue means anticlockwise, green means clockwise. On the correct turn it becomes red during the last ten divisions before the stop. Earlier passes stay blue or green. Bring the dot to the index. When the correct stop settles, the next target appears in the opposite direction’s colour; reverse and continue. The final red stop prompts Try handle, with an outline on the handle. Jennifer gives a time-of-day greeting and explains the dots, then calls the direction and appearance count. On red she says “Go anti slow” or “Go clock slow”, followed by “Single clicks”. At a verified stop she confirms the wheel and the next move. The final stop prompts the handle; a real opening triggers congratulations. A failed move or blocked handle receives the reset message once. Starting to turn during the introduction skips to the current command. Mechanical sounds still work, and Voice off mutes Jennifer. The direction caption also identifies which way to turn. Reset restarts; Stop Dial Instructions or changing mode removes the dots.

Lock settings, gate positions and Dial Instructions

Lock Settings describe the gate configuration discovered by the safecracker, listed nearest to farthest: 4 / 3 / 2 / 1. Gate positions are the main-dial readings at the gates, written to two decimal places. Their appearance numbers count arrivals at those decimal positions during discovery. Dial Instructions use integer dial positions and their own appearance numbers: pass the gate and stop at the specified integer, then reverse, or try the handle on the final row. The conversion accounts for reset positions, drive offsets and slack.

After opening the daily lock there are two tasks: identify the four lock settings and discover the gate positions and appearance numbers starting anticlockwise from Reset. Choose Next challenge: find the combination from the celebration, or scroll below the dial. The owner’s reference stays hidden until both tasks are solved.

Task 1 — lock settings. Reset the dial, turn anticlockwise, and watch each wheel in the Lock Model. When that wheel’s own gate is near the top, its rounded whole-number wheel reading gives its integer lock setting. The label Setting readable allows half a dial division (1.8°) on either side; Gate centred still marks the exact centre. Record it in the matching Wheel 4 / 3 / 2 / 1 box. Each wheel reading starts at zero on Reset and follows that wheel’s actual motion; it is different from the main dial reading. The broader Gate aligned indication is sufficient to open but may be outside the half-division range needed to read a setting. Catch gate sounds helps you stop at exact crossings. Your entries are checked automatically.

Task 2 — gate positions and appearance numbers. Reset, begin anticlockwise, and set the farthest wheel 1 first, then reverse to set wheels 2, 3, and 4. During discovery, check only the wheel currently being observed. Other wheels may move or lose alignment; their recorded stops and appearance counts are kept. Half a division of tolerance applies to the wheel being observed. Discovery has two phases for each move: first establish its appearance count, then find the watched wheel’s gate position. Once established, the count is kept while you reverse or adjust the position; other wheels and drive contacts may move. Correction turns are excluded from that count. Record the stopping number for the uninterrupted opening move. If a correction leaves the dial loose or approaches from the opposite pickup face, the guide explains the actual dial reading and the direct stop to record separately. A fence drop is not required to collect the last discovery reading. After recording all stops, your entries are checked automatically. A few locks need an initial anticlockwise clearing move before the four settings. If so, the form includes a Clear row and explains the extra move. The completed form is checked automatically by physically replaying your instructions on a fresh copy without changing your live dial or indicators.

Optional AI walkthrough. Choose Guide me through these tasks. This resets the live mechanism for a new survey while keeping your form entries. The assistant explains the full procedure for wheel 4, then uses brief confirmations and asks you to repeat for wheels 3, 2 and 1. She waits for each setting to be readable and the dial to settle before giving the rounded integer to enter. Two gates can be readable at the same dial position within the allowed tolerance; in that case she says the next wheel is already readable and asks for its entry without another turn. Gate positions use two-decimal-place main-dial readings with half a division (1.8°) of positioning tolerance. Record the number where you actually stop; the checker allows the same tolerance plus two-decimal rounding. A reply box appears directly inside the Lock Model. Enter the observed value and tap Check and continue. She checks it immediately: a correct entry is copied to the main reply form and she moves to the next wheel; a wrong reply stays open with feedback. After the fourth setting, she checks the whole settings task automatically. After the settings pass, she asks you to press Reset and guides discovery of the anticlockwise opening stops. She explains the procedure on the first wheel, then gives brief confirmations, the next direction and wheel, and the number and count to enter. Spoken prompts omit repeated explanations about reversing; free adjustment remains available during discovery. Earlier recorded observations remain valid even if those wheels later move. For each gate observation, the model reply asks for the gate position and appearance number. Check and continue records your reply and advances; the final reply also tests the complete sequence automatically. She copies only values you submit and does not hold wheels. Keep the dial still while recording an opening stop. If it moves within the allowed range, the assistant temporarily hides the reply box, checks the new stop, and updates the instruction and answer together. If you pass a stop by more than half a division, the guide enters correction mode instead of requiring Reset. Turn either way to take up the real slack, regain the watched wheel and align its gate. The discovery guide accepts alignment without requiring the drive contacts to remain engaged. A small reversal alone may not move that wheel. The discovery guide never returns to an earlier wheel just because it moved; only the current observation is checked. Correction turns are practice and are not added to the appearance counts of the uninterrupted opening instructions. If a gate aligns from the opposite pickup face, the guide distinguishes that corrected dial reading from the original direct stop to enter. A real fence drop is acknowledged when it occurs. Stop task guidance lets you continue independently. Captions and progress work with voice off; your selected on-device voice is used when available.

After completing both daily tasks, the Dial Instructions reference shows integer positions beyond the gates, directions and appearance numbers. At each stop, reverse for the next row; on the final row try the handle. A separate expandable discovery record retains your accepted gate positions to two decimal places and their gate appearance numbers, including any clearing move. Save Dial Instructions downloads the integer pass-through instructions and the verified decimal gate-position sequence as JSON. Previously saved files still load and are converted to integer instructions when displayed. For a complete guided opening, choose Dial Open With AI alongside the main starting buttons. This replaces the former Practise Dial Instructions buttons in the reference and celebration. Challenge progress survives reset and switching locks during the current visit; a new daily lock starts a new challenge, and reloading starts a fresh visit.

“Fourth appearance” means the fourth arrival of the specified number at the pointer while continuously turning in the instructed direction. Pass it three times; stop on the fourth. It is not necessarily four complete turns. Appearance counts depend on the stops and slack; do not assume a universal 5–4–3–2 sequence.

Load a saved lock

Choose Load saved lock in the Room, then choose your saved JSON file. The title identifies a Saved lock instead of the Daily lock. It replaces the introductory lock in the Room for this visit. The mechanism starts reset and locked, with the ordinary Lock Model, saved dial instructions and Dial Open With AI already available because this lock was cracked previously. Open it using your known sequence or AI assistance, and watch the model as you practise. Today’s Daily lock remains a separate choice. Select the saved lock’s tile in the Room to return to it. After refreshing the browser, load the file again.

The JSON contains the lock settings, integer pass-through Dial Instructions and the verified decimal gate-position sequence. Appearance numbers for integer positions and gate positions are kept separate. Older saved-lock JSON without an integer instruction section still loads. Older HTML instruction files cannot be loaded. Invalid or incompatible JSON leaves your current lock unchanged.

Practise your opening sequence

To practise, reset and open the main lock with the Lock Model open. Watch the wheels, gates and fence while you follow your discovered sequence. You can also choose Dial Open With AI for a guided opening and reopen the Lock Model to watch the mechanism.

Gate catching for testing

Normal play keeps gate catching on without a checkbox. For a developer test with continuous uncaught movement, add ?gateCatch=off to the game address before any # fragment and reload. An on-screen Test mode notice identifies the override. Remove that query option or open the normal game link to restore gate catching; it is not saved as a preference. This changes movement at gate crossings, not mechanical sound settings or the lock combination.

Keep your preferred on-device voice under Your AI assistant’s voice.
